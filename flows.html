    How to read this

    Direction
    Each band runs from the customer who pays to the supplier who is paid. Bands are gross payments: a dollar Microsoft pays Foxconn for an AI rack reappears in Foxconn's payment to NVIDIA, and again in NVIDIA's payment to TSMC. Column totals therefore overlap and should not be added together.
    Scope
    Only flows between the 119 companies on the dashboard: purchases of chips, memory, wafers, tools, subsystems, materials, gases, IP, packaging, test and assembly. Revenue from untracked customers (Meta, Dell, CXMT and many others) is not shown, so a company's bands can add up to far less than its revenue.
    Period
    Confidence
    Disclosed — the payer or supplier reports the amount or share for this counterparty (named, or identified with near certainty). Derived — calculated from reported figures plus a stated assumption. Estimated — analyst estimates or modelling from market shares; treat as an order of magnitude.
    Layout
    Tiers follow the dashboard. Tiers 10, 8, 7 and 0–1 are split by role (brands vs. ODMs, OSATs vs. test equipment, substrates vs. ABF film, wafers vs. polysilicon) because each contains a customer–supplier step of its own. Flows within a group, or running against the chain (such as Samsung's phone business buying Snapdragon chips), are listed below the chart instead of being drawn.

    Every flow lists its basis and sources in the table. These are static research figures, not live market data; corrections are welcome.

    MONEY FLOWS — static estimates compiled from company filings, annual reports, earnings calls and industry research. Bands show payments between tracked companies only. Market values on the companies page are fetched live; these flows are not.
